# Degree-bound note

No applicable explicit generator-degree bound theorem was located, and none
is assumed. Standing hypotheses are characteristic zero and triangularity
of the derivation.

Related literature checked:

- Dufresne–Kohls on finite separating sets: "This paper gives the first
  explicit example of a finite separating set in an invariant ring which is
  not finitely generated, namely, for Daigle and Freudenburg's 5-dimensional
  counterexample to Hilbert's Fourteenth Problem." (abstract, 2009)
- Freudenburg monograph, Algebraic Theory of Locally Nilpotent Derivations,
  Encyclopaedia of Mathematical Sciences 136, Springer (2nd ed. 2017):
  no universal explicit generator-degree bound B(deg D, variables,
  nilpotency data) for kernels of triangular derivations was located.
- Van den Essen kernel results (Polynomial Automorphisms and the Jacobian
  Conjecture, Progress in Mathematics 190, Birkhaeuser, 2000): the
  local-slice kernel algorithm computes ker D conditionally on finite
  generation and states no closed-form degree bound. Maubach's companion
  truncation paper ("An algorithm to compute the kernel of a derivation up
  to a certain degree", J. Symbolic Computation 29 (2000), 959–970) bounds
  only the computed truncation degree, not a completeness bound.
- Blocking fact: no universal B can exist for triangular LND kernels in
  >= 5 variables. Daigle–Freudenburg, "A counterexample to Hilbert's
  Fourteenth Problem in dimension five", J. Algebra 221(2) (1999), 528–535
  (kernel not finitely generated in dimension 5); Freudenburg,
  "A counterexample to Hilbert's Fourteenth Problem in dimension six",
  Transform. Groups 5 (2000), 61–71. Triangular Ga-actions with
  non-finitely-generated invariant rings exist, so finite generation —
  a fortiori any explicit degree bound — fails in general.
- Bounds noted and set aside as inapplicable: Maubach, "Triangular monomial
  derivations on k[X1,X2,X3,X4] have kernel generated by at most four
  elements", J. Pure Appl. Algebra 153(2) (2000), 165–170, Theorem 3.2
  (bounds the number of generators, not degrees, and only in 4 variables);
  Derksen–Kemper finite-separating-set theorem (Computational Invariant
  Theory, Theorem 2.3.15) guarantees a finite separating set, not a
  generating set.

For the record: deg D_5 = 2 (quadratic), 6 variables (a_0..a_5), D_5
triangular and locally nilpotent (see `formalize/AdjQuad/Deriv.lean`).
No value of B is assumed anywhere in the formalization or the CAS checks.

Conclusion: generation is CAS-certified to the computed range with no
completeness bound claimed. Follow-ups: tighter bounds via a0-filtration
analysis, or an extended CAS range.
